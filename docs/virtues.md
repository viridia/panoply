# Virtues

A hero has four virtues, Justice, Mercy, Wit and Grit: spiritual and psychological qualities rather
than D&D's strength and intelligence, as the original FTA's bravery, luck and kindness were. Each
virtue has its own skill tree, mixing combat, social and utility skills.

That much is decided. The rest of this document is a proposal from one brainstorm, none of it
adopted: each rule is a candidate to try in play and to drop when play says so, and a design that
contradicts it needs no justification beyond being better.

The virtues are scored independently and never oppose one another. Justice and Mercy conflict in
some choices, such as letting the guilty escape a cruel punishment, and agree in others; a choice
that serves one virtue earns it a deed without costing the other. A single axis, as Mass Effect's
Paragon and Renegade, punishes mixed play and locks players into one lane early.

## The trees

- A tree mixes combat, social and utility skills, grouped by theme, not by kind. Justice holds
  Smite, Prosecute and Liberation alike.
- Each tree draws on a vocabulary: Justice's is the courtroom (Prosecute, Vindication, Recusal,
  Shackle, Execute). Mercy might find one in sanctuary or medicine, Wit in the trickster or the
  merchant, Grit in the siege or the last stand.
- Grit is something the hero is rather than does, which tends toward passive stat bonuses, the least
  fun kind of skill. Its skills come from what enduring lets the hero do for others: hold ground so
  someone is protected (Bulwark), rally the party (War Song).
- The trees are about the same size, so the four boughs balance on screen. A thin tree is filled
  with skills that do something, never with stat bonuses to match the count.
- A prerequisite is the reason the next skill exists, not a toll on the way to it: Prosecute before
  Execute (no sentence without a verdict), Shackle before Liberation. A social skill that is a
  prerequisite also does something in combat, so a fighter who must take it is not paying for
  nothing.
- faery's `design/talents.md` holds the first brainstorm of names and functions. Skills as assets
  are X53.

### Combat skills

Each virtue owns a kind of moment in combat, so its combat skills are opportunity actions
([combat.md](combat.md), The action bar), variations on "when this happens, this opens".

| Virtue  | Its moment                                  | Its payoff                             | Owns            |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Justice | an enemy harms an ally, or is brought low   | retribution, finishing                 | the circle      |
| Mercy   | someone is hurt, or an enemy is beaten      | healing, protecting, sparing           | —               |
| Wit     | an enemy telegraphs                         | reading, interrupting, deceiving       | support roles   |
| Grit    | the hero takes the hit and stays standing   | enduring, holding ground               | the comeback    |

- Justice punishes the henchman who steps in; Wit sees the man with the rope coming; Grit's Resolve
  drives the comeback.
- Sparing is Mercy's mechanic: Clemency on a beaten enemy makes them yield and lowers their allies'
  morale, so a Mercy build wins fights differently, not only with more healing.
- Opposed skills answer the same moment: Execute and Clemency face the same beaten enemy, and the
  hero's pick is itself a deed.
- Since each virtue fires in different situations, the virtues a party leans on decide which
  encounters suit it.
- A deep tree needs few ideas: four to six signature skills per virtue, and nodes that upgrade them
  (Retaliation II also answers harm to a bystander) as passive modifiers.

### Layout

The tree screen is one composed image, not a map to pan and zoom: four boughs of an art-nouveau
tree, Justice facing Mercy across one axis and Wit facing Grit across the other. Skills that answer
the same moment from two trees sit near the border between them.

## Deeds

- A deed is earned by a choice that serves a virtue, in dialogue or in combat: the merciful answer,
  the beaten enemy spared.
- A close contest earns one too, announced as "you have earned a battle scar". Closeness counts
  whether the contest was won or lost, and a contest can be a fight, a chase, a bluff or a lock
  picked while the guard approaches, wherever a scene is authored as one. A routine win earns
  nothing.
- A decision that backfired counts: the bluff that collapses, the spared enemy who returns.
  Experience comes from bad decisions, and failure that pays something gives the player a reason to
  play on rather than reload. It still costs something real, or failing becomes the cheapest way to
  earn.
- Using a gated option or a skill earns nothing. Deeds come only from moments any hero can meet, so
  a hero deep in a tree advances in it no faster than one choosing the same virtue without it.
- A deed is never repeatable on demand: once per encounter, or tied to a choice.
- Thresholds count a virtue's own deeds, never its share of all deeds, which would make each
  virtue's deeds dilute the others'.

## Progression

Panoply is middling in length, well short of an 80-hour epic, so progression leans casual: a few
skill decisions in the whole game, each one mattering, none needing arithmetic. Two models are
candidates, to be told apart in play:

- **Threshold offers.** Deeds are the only measure. When a virtue's deeds cross a threshold, the
  player picks one of two or three skills from its tree, as Hades offers boons. Each threshold in a
  tree is further than the last, which keeps any one tree from running away. Builds differ because
  offers come only from the virtues the player has acted on.
- **Deeds unlock, points pay.** Deeds open nodes; a single currency, earned from close contests,
  buys them, its price rising with the points already spent in that tree so the first nodes of every
  tree stay cheap. Social skills are granted at deed thresholds rather than bought, so they never
  compete with combat skills for points.

Thresholds and prices are fractions of what the content offers, tuned once its length is known.

## Gates

- A dialogue option is gated on a skill, never on a virtue score, and any member of the party with
  the skill passes it.
- A follower owns a few skills from whichever trees fit their character, and stands for no single
  virtue.
- A follower's lines that come from who they are, rather than what they can do, are authored for
  that follower, outside the gates.
